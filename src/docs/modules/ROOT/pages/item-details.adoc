= Item Details
include::_attributes.adoc[]
:description: How to read the cover art, ratings, and metadata on an item's detail screen, and how to edit it, add it to a shelf, or refresh its cover art and metadata from there.

Every item in your collection has its own detail screen, showing everything MyMediaScanner knows about it and giving you the actions to change it.
This page walks through what you see there, top to bottom, and what each toolbar action does.

== Opening an item

Tap an item in xref:browsing.adoc[Browsing Your Collection] — or, on a narrow window or a phone, in a shelf — to open its detail screen.
On a wide desktop window the same information can appear instead in a side panel next to the collection list, without leaving the list view; see xref:browsing.adoc[Browsing Your Collection] for that layout.

image::screenshots/ios/item-detail.png[Item detail screen on a phone, showing the cover art, star rating, and metadata sections, role=screenshot, width=360]

== Cover art, title, and your rating

At the top of the screen is the item's cover art, shown large and centred with rounded corners.
If no cover art has been found yet, a placeholder icon is shown in its place — see <<Fetching cover art>> below.

Below the cover art is the title, and a subtitle if the item has one, such as an artist or director name.
Underneath that sits a five-star rating control: tap a star to set your own personal rating for the item, from one to five stars.
This rating is entirely your own opinion and is never fetched from anywhere.

If the item's metadata included a critic score, it appears just below your star rating as a score out of ten, together with the name of the source that supplied it — for example TMDB, Discogs, or Google Books, depending on the type of item.
Critic scores come from the same online lookup used when the item was first scanned or added, so not every item will have one; when a score is not available, nothing is shown in its place.
See <<Star rating vs critic score>> below for how the two relate to each other.

== Further down the page

Below the rating and critic score, in order, you may find:

* Any tags attached to the item.
* A free-text personal review, if one has been written.
* Purchase information and a current estimated value.
* The item's physical location — see xref:organising.adoc[Organising Your Collection].
* Progress tracking, if you are partway through the item — see xref:progress.adoc[Tracking Progress].
* Lending status, if the item is currently on loan — see xref:lending.adoc[Lending Tracker].
* For music items only, a rip status section showing whether the album has been matched to a ripped copy in your rip library — see xref:rip-library.adoc[Rip Library] for how that matching works.

Not every section appears for every item; a section is only shown when it has something to show.

== The metadata section

At the bottom of the screen is a metadata section with three parts:

Description:: The item's description or synopsis, if one was found.
Details:: Format, publisher, year, barcode, and genres, plus a few fields specific to the type of item — for example runtime for a film, or page count for a book.
Sources:: Which online services the item's metadata came from.

== Toolbar actions

The top of the screen offers actions for adding the item to a shelf, refreshing its metadata, fetching cover art, editing it, and deleting it.

NOTE: On a narrow phone screen, only *Edit* stays directly visible in the toolbar.
The shelf, refresh, and cover-art actions — and, on the very narrowest screens, delete — collapse into a "more actions" overflow menu so the title has room to breathe.
This is purely a space-saving layout choice; every action is still available, just one tap further away.

=== Editing an item

Tap *Edit* to open a form pre-filled with the item's current details.
Change any field you like, and optionally re-run the online lookup from within the form to pull in fresh values before saving.
Saving shows a confirmation and returns you to the item detail screen.

=== Fetching cover art

The *Fetch cover art* action appears only when the item currently has no cover art.
It searches online sources for artwork and reports back one of three outcomes: cover art updated, no cover art found, or — as a safeguard — that the item already has cover art.

=== Refreshing metadata

*Refresh metadata* re-runs the item's original online lookup and updates its stored details with whatever comes back.
A brief status message is shown while the lookup runs, and the app reports whether it succeeded or, if the lookup failed, shows an error.

TIP: Refreshing is useful when a metadata source has since corrected or expanded its entry for the item, for instance adding a critic score that was not yet available when you first scanned it.

=== Adding to a shelf

*Add to shelf* opens a picker so you can add the item to one or more shelves.
See xref:organising.adoc[Organising Your Collection] for what shelves are and how to manage them.

=== Deleting an item

*Delete* removes the item from your collection, after a confirmation prompt.
Deletion is soft: the item moves to Trash rather than being erased outright, so it can still be restored — see xref:browsing.adoc[Browsing Your Collection] for how.

== Star rating vs critic score

The star rating and the critic score can look similar at a glance, but they measure different things and never influence one another.

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| | Star rating | Critic score

| Whose opinion is it?
| Yours
| An external source

| Scale
| One to five stars
| Out of ten

| Where it comes from
| You tap a star
| Fetched automatically with the item's metadata

| Always present?
| Yes, once you set it
| No — absent for niche or very new releases
|===

Changing your star rating never affects the critic score, and a low (or missing) critic score has no bearing on the rating you choose to give the item yourself.
