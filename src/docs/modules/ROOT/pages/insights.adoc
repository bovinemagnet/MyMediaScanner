= Insights
include::_attributes.adoc[]
:description: See statistics about your collection, and export it as CSV, JSON, or a browsable static website.

The Insights screen turns your collection into charts and headline numbers, and is also where you export your data.
On desktop it has its own sidebar entry; on mobile it is one of the main bottom navigation tabs.

image::screenshots/ios/insights.png[Insights screen with collection statistics,role=screenshot,width=360]

== What you will see

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Section | What it shows

| Headline stats
| Total items catalogued, how many media types are represented, your average rating, and how many items you have rated.

| Collection growth
| A chart of how your collection has grown over time.

| Collection value
| The combined price paid across your owned items, if you record prices.

| Genre and media type breakdown
| A bar chart of your most common genres alongside a pie chart of your collection by media type.

| Items by year
| A bar chart of how many items you own from each release year.
|===

A time period selector at the top of the screen lets you restrict the growth chart to a shorter window.

== If you track purchase value

Once you have recorded a price on at least one owned item, three extra cards appear: your top value items, a value breakdown by media type, and a value-over-time timeline.
Alongside them, a *Valuation report* lets you export an insurance-style report — one row per priced item plus totals by media type — as a CSV file or a formatted HTML page.
A *Refresh all current values* action is also offered there, which re-checks the current market value of eligible music items in your collection.

== Lending and rip library snapshots

If you have any loan history, a lending summary appears showing active and overdue loans and your most frequent borrowers — see xref:lending.adoc[Lending].
If you have a rip library configured, a coverage card appears alongside it — see xref:rip-library.adoc[Rip Library].
Either card is hidden until you have data for it to show.

== Exporting your collection

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Format | What you get

| CSV
| A spreadsheet-friendly file with one row per item and columns for title, media type, genres, rating, price, and more.

| JSON
| The same data in a structured file, useful for feeding into another tool.
|===

Both buttons live in the *Export* section of the Insights screen.
Deleted (trashed) items are never included in either export.
Once the export finishes, the app shows you the exact path of the file it wrote.

== Static website export

For a portable, browsable copy of your whole collection, use the static HTML export instead: open *Settings > Export > Static HTML export*.

image::screenshots/desktop/settings.png[Settings screen, home of the static HTML export,role=screenshot,width=720]

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Option | What it does

| Site title
| The heading shown at the top of the exported site.

| Private tag
| Items carrying this tag are left out of the export entirely — useful for anything you would rather not include if you share the export.

| Download and bundle cover art
| Saves a local copy of every cover image so the exported site still shows artwork when viewed offline.
|===

Choose a destination folder and the export writes a searchable, filterable website — a grid of your collection plus a page per item — that you can open directly in a browser, drop into a static hosting service such as Netlify Drop or GitHub Pages, or serve from your own web server.

NOTE: Deleted (trashed) items are never included in the static export, and items tagged with your chosen private tag are left out as well — neither their covers nor their titles appear anywhere in the exported files.
